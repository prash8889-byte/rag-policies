1. Hiring and onboarding
Recruitment and selection: Defines how vacancies are approved, advertised, screened, interviewed, and offered. It covers who approves headcount, the interview stages, and how offers are made, so hiring is consistent and merit-based.
Equal opportunity and non-discrimination: Commits the company to hiring and treating people fairly regardless of gender, religion, caste, disability, age, or marital status. It usually includes how discrimination complaints are handled.
Background verification: Specifies the checks done before or after joining (education, past employment, criminal record, address) and what happens if a candidate fails or hides information. It also requires candidate consent.
Probation and confirmation: Sets the trial period (typically 3 to 6 months), what is evaluated, how it can be extended, and how confirmation is communicated. It also states notice terms during probation.
Onboarding and induction: Covers the joining formalities (documents, ID, IT access, assets) and the orientation program that introduces the company, team, policies, and role expectations.
2. Working hours and attendance
Work hours, shifts, and overtime: States standard working hours, shift timings, break rules, weekly offs, and how overtime is approved and paid or compensated, in line with labor laws.
Attendance and punctuality: Explains how attendance is recorded (biometric, app, timesheet), grace periods for late arrival, and consequences of repeated lateness or unauthorized absence.
Remote and hybrid work: Defines who is eligible to work remotely, how many days, expected availability, equipment and internet support, and data security requirements at home.
Flexible working arrangements: Allows adjusted start/end times, compressed weeks, or part-time options, usually requiring manager approval and business feasibility.
3. Leave and time off
Casual, sick, and earned leave: Sets the annual entitlement for each type, how it accrues, whether it carries forward, and how to apply. Sick leave often needs a medical certificate beyond 2 to 3 days.
Maternity, paternity, and adoption leave: Maternity leave in India is 26 weeks for the first two children under the Maternity Benefit Act. Paternity and adoption leave are often company-specific. The policy also covers eligibility and return-to-work support.
Bereavement and compassionate leave: Paid time off on the death or serious illness of a close family member, with a defined list of relatives covered.
Public and optional holidays: Publishes the annual holiday calendar and a set number of optional or floating holidays employees can choose for regional or religious occasions.
Leave without pay and sabbatical: Allows unpaid or long breaks for study, health, or personal reasons, with rules on eligibility, maximum duration, and job protection.
Leave encashment: Explains whether unused leave can be paid out (annually or at exit), the maximum days, and how the amount is calculated.
4. Compensation and benefits
Salary structure and pay cycle: Breaks pay into components (basic, HRA, allowances, variable) and states the payment date, deductions (tax, PF), and how pay slips are issued.
Bonuses and incentives: Describes performance bonuses, sales incentives, joining or retention bonuses, who qualifies, how they are calculated, and clawback conditions.
Provident fund, gratuity, ESI: Statutory benefits in India. PF is a retirement savings scheme with employee and employer contributions. Gratuity is a lump sum after 5 years of service. ESI gives medical and cash benefits to lower-wage employees.
Health and life insurance: Details group medical and term cover, sum insured, dependents covered, claim process, and whether it continues after exit.
Allowances: Lists extras such as travel, meals, phone, internet, and relocation, with eligibility and limits.
Expense reimbursement: Explains what business expenses can be claimed, required receipts, approval limits, and submission deadlines.
5. Performance and growth
Performance appraisal and review cycles: Sets the review frequency (annual, half-yearly, quarterly), rating scale, goal-setting method, and how ratings feed into pay and promotion.
Promotions and increments: States the criteria, timing, and approval process for raises and level changes, so decisions are transparent.
Training and development: Covers learning budgets, mandatory training, certifications, tuition support, and bonds or repayment if an employee leaves soon after sponsored training.
Career progression and succession planning: Maps career paths and identifies and grooms people for key roles so the company isn’t caught short when someone leaves.
6. Code of conduct and ethics
Employee code of conduct: The overall behavior standard: integrity, respect, honesty, professionalism, and compliance with law.
Conflict of interest: Requires employees to disclose situations where personal interests (side businesses, family relationships with vendors) could affect their judgment.
Confidentiality and data protection: Protects trade secrets, client data, and personal data, setting rules for sharing, storing, and handling information, often tied to an NDA and data privacy law.
Gifts and hospitality: Limits accepting or giving gifts and entertainment to prevent bribery or undue influence, and requires reporting above a certain value.
Dress code: Sets expectations for attire (formal, business casual, casual days), including any safety or client-facing requirements.
Use of company assets, internet, and email: Defines acceptable use of laptops, phones, networks, and accounts, and states that the company may monitor usage on its systems.
Social media policy: Guides what employees may post about the company, colleagues, or clients, and prohibits sharing confidential information or speaking on behalf of the company without authorization.
Whistleblower policy: Provides a safe, confidential channel to report fraud, misconduct, or legal violations, with protection against retaliation.
7. Workplace safety and respect
Prevention of sexual harassment (POSH): Mandatory in India for workplaces with 10 or more employees. It defines harassment, requires an Internal Committee, and sets the complaint, inquiry, and action process.
Anti-bullying and anti-harassment: Covers non-sexual harassment such as intimidation, humiliation, and discrimination, with a reporting and investigation process.
Health and safety and emergency procedures: Covers hazard prevention, first aid, fire drills, evacuation plans, incident reporting, and ergonomic or industrial safety rules.
Substance abuse: Prohibits working under the influence of alcohol or drugs and bringing them to work, and may include testing and support or rehabilitation.
Workplace violence: Prohibits threats, physical aggression, and weapons, and sets out how incidents are reported and handled.
8. Discipline and grievances
Disciplinary procedure: Sets the steps from verbal warning to written warning, show-cause notice, inquiry, and final action, ensuring natural justice (the right to be heard).
Grievance redressal: Gives employees a structured way to raise complaints about managers, pay, or conditions, with escalation levels and response timelines.
Misconduct definitions and consequences: Lists what counts as minor or serious misconduct (fraud, absenteeism, insubordination, data theft) and the penalties for each.
Warning and termination procedures: Explains how performance or conduct issues lead to termination, including notice, documentation, and approvals, to reduce legal risk.
9. Separation
Resignation and notice period: States how to resign, the notice length (commonly 1 to 3 months), buyout or waiver options, and handover duties.
Termination and retrenchment: Covers company-initiated exits, whether for cause, performance, or layoffs, including notice, severance, and legal compliance.
Full and final settlement: Explains how final dues (salary, leave encashment, bonus, gratuity, less recoveries) are calculated and when they are paid, typically within 30 to 45 days.
Exit interview and clearance: Describes the feedback conversation and the no-dues process: returning assets, revoking access, and clearing departments.
Experience and relieving letters: States when and how these documents are issued, usually after clearance and settlement.
Non-compete and non-solicitation: Restricts joining competitors or poaching employees or clients after exit. Enforceability varies, and in India post-employment non-competes are generally hard to enforce.
10. Other common policies
Employee referral program: Rewards employees for referring candidates who are hired and stay for a set period, with rules on bonus amount and eligibility.
Travel policy: Defines who can travel, class of travel, hotel and per-diem limits, booking process, and advance and settlement rules.
Transfer and relocation: Covers moving employees between locations, including notice, relocation allowance, temporary housing, and refusal consequences.
Employee wellness and mental health (EAP): Offers counseling, health checkups, wellness programs, and confidential support through an Employee Assistance Program.
Diversity, equity, and inclusion: Sets goals and practices for an inclusive workplace, such as fair hiring, accessibility, and employee resource groups.
Moonlighting and outside employment: States whether employees may take second jobs or freelance work, usually requiring disclosure and approval and banning conflicts with the employer’s business.
Intellectual property ownership: Clarifies that work created in the course of employment (code, designs, content, inventions) belongs to the company
